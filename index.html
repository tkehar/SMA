About the Archive

Sabeen Mahmud was a human rights activist, entrepreneur and design enthusiast who stood for community, culture and open dialogue. In 2007 she founded The Second Floor (T2F) a café and community space.

In April 2015 Sabeen hosted an event titled “Unsilencing Balochistan” opening a discourse on Balochistan’s disappeared people. Following this Sabeen was shot at a signal as she was leaving the event, cutting short a life that had impacted and changed so many.

This archive allows viewers to get to know Sabeen Mahmud and her work through virtual reconstructions of spaces she inhabited. Explore these spaces to discover the stories behind artefacts and memories from her friends, colleagues, and family.

Curatorial Note

The act of archiving raises questions of truth, representation, experience, inheritance and discovery. This archive is not a library where knowledge is cataloged and lent, nor is it a museum where artefacts are exoticized. Rather it is a collection of memory, moments and reflections collected since 2015. It is a labor of love brought to fruition by a various actors who have worked asynchronously over this period. I met Sabeen Mahmud in 2014 when she hired me as an intern, that internships became a job, that job led me to a community, and that community has created lifelong connections. This is a sentiment that holds true for many who knew, worked with, or interacted with Sabeen.

Following her assassination this community was devastated and in many ways still is. This archive is an attempt to process that devastation and loss. It is also a reflection on inheritance, what was learned, what was gained, and what will be carried forward.

The archival process creates an intense relationship between the archivist, the archival documents, and the archival subject. What became evident in this process is the complexity of authentic representation. The archivist is always implicated in their choices; selecting what to include and how to present that selection frames the politics of the archive. The documents are imbued with a life of their own, having a typology, narrative and a performative quality. Spatial context is not just vital; it is constitutive as an artefact outside it’s context loses meaning, intimacy and becomes a specimen. This archive challenges that detachment through virtual re/constructions of spaces that are designed to situate the viewer in the emotional context that these artefacts inhabit.

Creating this archive also means acknowledging it’s incompleteness. With its subject having no hand in the process and being only referred to or implied. This cannot be a chronology of life or a biographic retelling, rather it is a living archive that I invite you to explore, critique and contribute to. There is no clear journey in this archive rather it is about discovery and curiosity. It is about understanding and knowing Sabeen.
